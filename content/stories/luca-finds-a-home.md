When our field coordinator first met **Luca**, he was pressed into the back corner of a public-shelter run, sharing the space with more dogs than it was built for. He didn't bark. He just watched.

## The pull

We pulled Luca that week. A vet check, his first proper meal, and a foster place in a quiet flat where he could decompress away from the noise. For the first few days he wouldn't take food from a hand. By the end of the month, he was leaning into every scratch.

## Learning to be a dog

Fostering gave Luca the thing a shelter never can: time, and a person. He learned the lead, learned that doors open and close without danger, learned to sleep on his back with his paws in the air.

> He arrived not knowing how to be a pet. He left knowing nothing else.

## Home

A family in Munich saw his profile, applied, and passed their home check with room to spare. After his vaccinations, passport and a licensed transport north, Luca stepped into a garden for the first time — and did three full laps before flopping into the grass.

One dog, one home. And in the Bucharest shelter, one more space for the next dog who needs it.
